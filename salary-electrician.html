ELECTRICIAN SALARY AUSTRALIA 2025–26

NewFinera · Updated August 2026 · General education only

Electrical work in Australia is licensed at the state level, not nationally — meaning an electrician's earning potential is shaped as much by which state regulator they're registered with (NSW Fair Trading, Energy Safe Victoria, Queensland's Electrical Safety Office, and equivalents elsewhere) as by their years of experience. Demand has also shifted noticeably toward renewable energy work: solar PV installation and EV charger fit-outs are now common specialisations that didn't exist as trade categories a decade ago.

SALARY RANGE BY CAREER STAGE

Apprentice electricians are paid on a rising scale across their 4-year Certificate III training, typically starting well below award wages for qualified tradespeople and increasing at each year milestone. Once licensed, most fully qualified electricians earn in the $75,000–$100,000 range as employees. Those who go out on their own ABN as contractors, or who move into specialised industrial, commercial, or renewables work, commonly report $110,000–$150,000+, though self-employed income is gross of GST, insurance, vehicle, and tooling costs that an employed electrician doesn't carry.

WHAT ACTUALLY MOVES ELECTRICIAN PAY

  • State licensing: licence fees and renewal requirements differ by state, and self-employed electricians typically carry this cost themselves rather than having an employer absorb it
  • Penalty rates: after-hours callouts, shutdown work, and weekend or public holiday jobs under electrical contracting awards can add substantial premiums on top of the base hourly rate for employed electricians
  • Specialisation: solar PV installation, EV charging infrastructure, and data/communications cabling are attracting higher day rates as renewable energy and connectivity demand grows faster than the licensed workforce
  • FIFO and remote allowances: electricians working fly-in-fly-out or residentially in mining and resources regions often receive allowances that lift total pay well above metro award rates
  • Contracting vs employment: running your own ABN increases gross billings but adds GST, public liability insurance, vehicle, and quoting overheads that reduce the effective margin compared to a wage

CAREER PROGRESSION

After licensing, a common path runs from general residential/commercial electrician through to a specialised trade (industrial automation, solar, or data/comms), then either supervisory roles with a larger contractor or a jump to running an independent electrical contracting business — which raises earning ceiling but introduces business risk, quoting time, and irregular cash flow that a wage doesn't carry.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How long does it take to become a qualified electrician in Australia?

Typically a 4-year Certificate III apprenticeship combining paid on-the-job training with TAFE study, followed by obtaining a state electrical licence.

Do electricians need a licence in every state they work in?

Yes — electrical licensing is state-based, and working across state lines generally requires mutual recognition registration or a separate licence application in that state.

Is overtime pay common for electricians?

Yes. Many employed electricians are covered by penalty rates for after-hours callouts, weekend work, and public holidays under relevant modern awards or enterprise agreements, which can meaningfully lift annual earnings above the base rate.
