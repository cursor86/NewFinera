SOLICITOR SALARY AUSTRALIA 2025–26

NewFinera Editorial Team · Updated August 2026 · General education only

A law degree alone doesn't make you a practising solicitor in Australia — admission also requires completing Practical Legal Training (PLT). Once admitted, the single biggest driver of pay isn't seniority alone but firm tier: national and top-tier commercial firms routinely pay graduate lawyers substantially more than suburban or regional practices, generally in exchange for higher billable hour expectations.

SALARY RANGE BY CAREER STAGE

Graduate solicitors at top-tier and national firms often start around $85,000–$100,000, while suburban and regional firms may start closer to $60,000–$75,000. Mid-level solicitors (3-6 years PQE) commonly earn $110,000–$150,000 depending on firm and practice area. Senior associates typically reach $150,000–$200,000, and the jump to salaried or equity partner — where income becomes tied to firm profitability and personal client base — can substantially exceed that, though it varies enormously by firm.

WHAT ACTUALLY MOVES SOLICITOR PAY

  • Firm tier: national and top-tier commercial firms typically pay graduate and junior lawyers substantially more than suburban, regional, or boutique practices, though often with higher billable hour expectations in return
  • Billable hour targets and bonuses: many private practice roles set annual billable hour targets, with bonuses tied to exceeding them — this can significantly affect total compensation beyond base salary, alongside longer working hours
  • In-house vs private practice: solicitors who move in-house to corporate legal teams after several years often trade a somewhat lower base salary for more predictable hours, though senior in-house counsel and general counsel roles can be highly paid
  • Practice area: corporate/M&A, banking and finance, and construction law solicitors typically command higher salaries than family law, criminal law, or community legal centre roles, reflecting client budgets and complexity
  • Partnership track: the step from senior associate to salaried partner, and eventually equity partner, represents the largest potential pay increase in a legal career, though it comes with significant business development responsibility

CAREER PROGRESSION

The typical path runs from Graduate/Law Graduate through Solicitor, Senior Associate, and then either Special Counsel or Partner (salaried, then potentially equity) at a private firm — or a lateral move to in-house counsel, government legal roles, or the bar as a barrister. Each branch trades off pay, hours, and autonomy differently, and many solicitors move between private practice and in-house roles more than once across a career.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is Practical Legal Training (PLT) and is it required?

PLT is a mandatory practical component required in Australia before a graduate can be admitted as a solicitor, typically completed through a College of Law or university-based program after finishing a Juris Doctor or Bachelor of Laws.

Do corporate lawyers earn more than criminal or family lawyers?

Generally yes — commercial practice areas like mergers and acquisitions, banking and finance, and major construction disputes tend to have larger client budgets and higher billing rates than family, criminal, or community legal work, which typically flows through to solicitor salaries.

How much does making partner increase a solicitor's income?

It can be substantial, particularly moving from salaried to equity partner, where income becomes tied to firm profitability and the partner's own client base rather than a fixed salary — though this varies enormously between firms and practice areas.
