SALARY RANGE BY CAREER STAGE

Graduate teachers typically start around $75,000–$80,000 on the base public school pay scale. Experienced classroom teachers who've reached the top of the standard scale commonly earn $90,000–$100,000. Highly Accomplished and Lead Teacher accreditation, or a move into leadership as Head of Department, can push pay to $105,000–$130,000, and Deputy Principal and Principal roles at larger schools frequently exceed that further.

WHAT ACTUALLY MOVES TEACHER PAY

  • Pay scale progression: teachers progress through incremental pay scales tied to years of experience and, in most states, formal AITSL-aligned accreditation levels (Proficient, Highly Accomplished, Lead Teacher)
  • Sector: public school pay follows standardised state award scales, while Catholic and independent school pay can vary more by individual school, sometimes exceeding public sector rates at senior levels
  • Leadership roles: moving into Head of Department, Deputy Principal, or Principal is the primary route to significantly higher pay, alongside a reduced classroom teaching load
  • Rural and remote incentives: state education departments offer relocation subsidies, incentive payments, and priority transfer arrangements to attract and retain teachers in hard-to-staff schools
  • Shortage-subject incentives: teachers qualified in shortage areas like mathematics, physics, and technology can sometimes access additional incentive payments or faster employment pathways

CAREER PROGRESSION

Most teachers begin as a Graduate Teacher, reach Proficient accreditation within their first few years, and then either pursue Highly Accomplished/Lead Teacher status to progress along the classroom teaching scale, or move into leadership via Head of Department, Deputy Principal, and eventually Principal — a track that shifts the role from classroom instruction to school-wide management.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Do private school teachers earn more than public school teachers?

It varies — public school pay follows standardised state award scales, while independent and Catholic school pay is set more individually per school; some well-resourced independent schools pay above public sector rates, particularly for experienced and leadership staff, though this isn't universal.

How do teachers progress to higher pay scales?

Beyond standard years-of-service increments, most Australian states use AITSL-aligned accreditation levels, where achieving Highly Accomplished or Lead status through a formal certification process can unlock higher pay bands.

Is there a shortage-subject pay incentive for teachers?

Some state education departments offer incentive payments or accelerated placement for teachers qualified in shortage subjects like mathematics, physics, and technology, though the specifics vary by state and change periodically based on workforce needs.
